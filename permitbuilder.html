Permit to Work - Digitalisation


Digitising the process of permit to work issuance for the fieldwork teams of SLB to ensure safety and compliance while improving efficiency and transparency.

Project details

  • Category:Work Project
  • Year: 2024
  • Expertise:Interface Design, Responsive Design
  • Area:Digital Form, Workflow Automation

For better safety, and safer environment

Understanding the Problem

In safety-critical environments, all field activities such as installation, repair, or maintenance require formal permits to ensure worker and environmental safety. At SLB, this process was historically managed through manual workflows involving physical forms, supervisor consultations, and multiple approval layers.

It creates many problems -

1. More time consumption

2. Higher chances of human error

3. Displacement or missing documents

4. Redundant data filling in physical forms

5. Effort to organise these forms and send to relevent places

6. less scope of writing assistance for workers

Problem Statement

Users face delays and friction due to generic, step-heavy permit workflows. The goal is to create a streamlined digital experience that adapts to the specific permit type, reducing redundant inputs and approval bottlenecks.

Solution Overview

The manual workflow was transformed into a step-based digital experience that progressively reveals information, reducing cognitive load. Key elements—form fields, on-ground safety checks, attachments, interlinked permits, and approval flows—were unified into a cohesive system. This allowed users to build a complete mental model of the permit before submission, while giving administrators real-time visibility and control over the process.

The key design implementations were:

Restructuring the form

The form was restructured to reduce cognitive load and improve the user experience.

Transparent Approval Process

The visibility into the approval process allowed users to track the status of their permit requests in real-time.

Mobile Responsive Design

Ensuring the application provides an optimal user experience across all devices specially mobile devices.

Digitisation vs Digitalisation

The Permit restructuring was a transformative process, not just a translation of the existing form into digital format. It involved rethinking the entire user journey and redesigning the interface to better suit the needs of the users and the context of use.

The physical form had had a complex structure with multiple sections and redundant fields.

A more logical approach to structuring the permit application was implemented, reducing ambiguity and making it more intuitive for users. The 7 step process now provides a clearer path for users to follow.

It starts with filling out basic information

There are a total of 7 Permits in this process, they are defined by the type of Work. For example repairing a pipe at a height comes under permit type “Work At Height”. This has been a pain for new workers to check the Permit Work every time to verify. The new UI has been designed a such a way that permit selection is not a headache of users, user selects what job they are doing and permits get auto selected based on that, keeping override options intact.

Permit Specific Details are visible for only selected permits, this is where we eliminate lot of mental overload on users. You just fill the things you need.

Once all the sections of the form has been filled, users can review and submit their applications. This view is same from this point onwards and Authority and Experts also see this view of the final application.

Visibility of Approval Process

The visibility into the approval process need to be transparent to users, ensuring they can track the status of their requests in real-time. This also help the administration in managing and responding to requests more efficiently.

The approval process being multidirectional and multistep often leads to confusions and difficult to track both from permit holders and approvers. Also an another level adds up when expert approval also comes in the picture.

After mapping the approval flow, the challenge was to seamlessly integrate it with the UI. I Focused on two aspects mainly - 1. Tranparency 2. Keeping the Chain of events clear to understand

And to do that I have created layout that keeps the permit always visible for refernence and a section for all apporval related information and Actions.

On the Go!

Enabling users to create, access, and manage permits from anywhere was a key business priority to improve accessibility. However, moving the process online alone would not be sufficient without delivering a seamless mobile experience.

Instead of designing for screens, we designed for continuity across contexts.

CSS Flexbox and Grids

The product was designed to work seamlessly across both mobile and desktop, not as an afterthought, but as a parallel priority from the outset. Instead of following a strict mobile-first or desktop-first approach, the experience was crafted simultaneously for both contexts. The layout system was built with responsiveness at its core. To do that design decisions were closely aligned with implementation using flexible layout principles, particularly leveraging CSS Flexbox and Grid. This translated into a predominantly linear and modular structure, allowing content to reflow naturally without breaking hierarchy or usability.

Adaptive UI Elements

To address the contrast between limited mobile space and expansive desktop views, adaptive UI patterns were used. On smaller screens, components like the stepper collapeses, some components hide secordary informations like icons to reduce cognitive load. On desktop, the same components expand to provide richer context and visibility, enabling faster scanning and informed decision-making.

Navigation was treated as a constant anchor across devices. Key elements such as the stepper and the bottom action bar remained persistently accessible, ensuring users always know where they are and what to do next. Also the design accounted for real-world usage—particularly in field environments. Touch-friendly interactions, clear visual hierarchy, and minimized input friction were prioritized to support quick, error-free actions on mobile.

Reflection

Not every digitization effort requires reinvention — some benefit more from thoughtful optimization

Working at SLB within an agile studio, I’ve often translated existing processes into digital experiences. This project reinforced an important distinction: not every digitization effort requires reinvention—some benefit more from thoughtful optimization. Meaningful impact doesn’t always come from disruption, but often from subtle, well-informed improvements.

While the business goal was clear—“convert these forms into an online platform”—this could have easily resulted in a direct, one-to-one replication of the manual process. However, I learned that as designers, our role goes beyond execution; it is to surface possibilities.
